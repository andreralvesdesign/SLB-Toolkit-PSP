Suicide: recognising signs and seeking help

This sheet deals with suicidal ideation and suicide in the workplace. It is a sensitive and delicate subject. Go at your own pace and, if you prefer, come back later.

Situation 1 · A colleague with suicidal ideation

What may be happening?

  • The person may be in intense emotional distress, feeling hopeless, empty or without a way out.
  • They may feel isolated, misunderstood or a “burden” to others.
  • Their ability to think clearly may be reduced and they may have difficulty identifying or expressing emotional needs.
  • Demanding personal or professional situations may contribute to a state of great emotional dysregulation.

Situation 1 · A colleague with suicidal ideation

What to do?

  1. 1
    Establish contact and presence: Approach calmly and respectfully. “Can I stay with you for a while?”
  2. 2
    Ensure safety: Take the person to a quieter space and reduce stimuli and pressure.
  3. 3
    Assess needs: Ask in a simple way, for example, “What is hardest for you right now?”
  4. 4
    Listen with empathy: Listen without interrupting, judging or correcting. “I imagine this is weighing on you heavily.”
  5. 5
    Support immediate needs: Presence, water, helping to contact someone. Small gestures make a difference.
  6. 6
    Connect with resources: Encourage professional help and offer concrete help (e.g. accompanying them, making contact easier).
  7. 7
    Promote hope: “You don’t have to deal with this alone”, “There is support available”.

Situation 1 · A colleague with suicidal ideation

What to avoid and when to refer

What to avoid?
  • Forcing the person to talk or open up.
  • Minimising (“this will pass”).
  • Judging or trying to “teach a lesson”.
  • Pressing for quick solutions.
  • Ignoring signs out of discomfort.
  • Promising absolute confidentiality when there is a risk.
When to refer?
  • Whenever there is suicidal ideation (direct or indirect)
  • Whenever there are indications of a plan or risk
  • When the person cannot guarantee their own safety

Situation 2 · When a suicide occurs in the workplace

What may be happening?

  • The team may be in shock or emotionally disorganised.
  • Emotions such as guilt, sadness, confusion and fear may be present.
  • A feeling of a lack of safety in the workplace.
  • A need for safety, stability and meaning.
  • Varied reactions, but all valid.

Do you need help now?1121411 Suicide Prevention808 24 24 24 SNS 24

Situation 2 · When a suicide occurs in the workplace

What to do?

  1. 1
    Establish presence and safe communication: Clear, respectful and simple communication. Show availability.
  2. 2
    Ensure emotional safety: Reduce rumours and uncertainty. Create an environment of respect and non-judgement.
  3. 3
    Assess needs: Find out who is most affected. Ask “How are you coping with this situation?”
  4. 4
    Support immediate needs: Flexibility, breaks, practical support. Presence and support between colleagues.
  5. 5
    Connect with resources: Provide psychological support. Make it easier to access help.
  6. 6
    Promote hope and normalisation: “It’s natural to have many different reactions”, “You don’t have to go through this alone”.

Situation 2 · When a suicide occurs in the workplace

What to avoid and when to refer

What to avoid?
  • Speculation or looking for who is to blame.
  • Excessive details about the suicide.
  • Total silence or concealment.
  • Pressure to “get back to normal quickly”.
  • Forcing emotional sharing.
When to refer? When there is:
  • Intense or prolonged distress
  • Difficulty functioning day to day
  • Persistent guilt or disturbing thoughts

We should refer people for psychological support but also follow their progress over time, and not only in the immediate aftermath.

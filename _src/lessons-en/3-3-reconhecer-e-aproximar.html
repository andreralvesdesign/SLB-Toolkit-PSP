Recognising signs

What to observe

A colleague will not always say directly that they are in distress. Often, what appears are subtle changes in their usual behaviour.

You may notice:

  • More irritability, impatience or reactivity
  • Withdrawal or less interaction with the team
  • Difficulty concentrating or more mistakes
  • A drop in motivation or involvement
  • Changes in work pace (slower or faster)
  • Frequent expressions of tiredness, frustration or overload

More than isolated signs, what is most relevant is the change

“Is this person different from how they usually are?”

“Is this behaviour new or more intense than before?”

Not every change means distress, but it can be an indication that something is not right.

Common doubts

It is natural to hesitate before approaching a colleague

Some frequent doubts
  • “What if I am misreading it?”
  • “What if they do not want to talk?”
  • “What if I say the wrong thing?”
  • “What if I make things worse?”

These doubts are understandable and part of the process.

However, it is important to keep in mind
  • You do not need to be certain to approach someone
  • You do not need the perfect words
  • You do not need to know how to “fix it”

What matters most is the intention: showing availability and care. In most situations, a respectful approach does no harm and can make a significant difference.

Action guide

Should I approach them?

You do not need to wait for extreme signs before approaching someone. On the contrary, early interventions are more effective and less intrusive.

Action guide · acting with priority

Signs that need more attention

If you observe any of these signs, it is important not to delay:

  • !Very marked changes in behaviour
  • !Expressions of extreme exhaustion or hopelessness
  • !Clear difficulty functioning day to day
  • !Signs of risk to themselves or to others

In these cases

It may be important not to leave the person alone and to seek additional support.

Key message

If something in you has the feeling that you should ask, you probably already have enough information to approach them.
